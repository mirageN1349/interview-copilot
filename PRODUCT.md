# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People using a macOS interview assistant to prepare a profile, start a meeting, receive live help, work with code or diagrams, and review previous sessions. The visible workflow should feel like a conventional desktop product and must not expose internal authorization, audit, or test-environment terminology.

## Product Purpose

Reproduce the consumer-facing behavior of an interview copilot in authorized controlled tests so detection signals can be evaluated without exposing real candidates or non-consenting third parties.

## Brand Personality

Precise, discreet, native. The interface should feel like a well-made macOS utility: calm under pressure, dense without clutter, and explicit about security state.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI dashboards with purple gradients, glowing orbs, oversized prompt boxes, and decorative assistant avatars.
- Glass applied to every card or nested translucent panels that reduce hierarchy and contrast.
- Neon cyber-security styling, fake terminal motifs, or theatrical hacker visuals.
- AI-generated icons, emoji as interface icons, mixed icon families, and copied third-party application identities.
- Web controls that imitate macOS visually but behave unlike native controls.

## Design Principles

- The normal product surface uses familiar labels: Home, Profiles, Meeting, Live, Ask, System Design, History, Settings, and Account.
- Internal authorization, audit, consent, and test-build controls stay out of normal navigation and copy; they are enforced out of band and fail closed.
- Capture, model, profile, monitor, recording, and connection state remain clear to the user.
- Keyboard-first means every workflow, not a shortcut layer over mouse-only controls.
- Native behavior outranks decoration; glass is reserved for the floating overlay and structural chrome where material communicates layering.
- Dense evidence stays readable through typography, alignment, and progressive disclosure rather than card grids.
- The product can reproduce realistic interaction patterns without copying another product's brand, artwork, identity, or unsupported marketing claims.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA where web content applies. Support complete keyboard operation, visible focus, VoiceOver labels, reduced motion, sufficient contrast in light and dark appearances, non-color status cues, and scalable text without clipping critical controls.
